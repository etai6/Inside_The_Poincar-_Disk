## What it is

The Poincaré disk is a model of the hyperbolic plane: the entire infinite plane is drawn inside a circle. Straight lines ("geodesics") appear as circular arcs that meet the boundary at right angles, or as diameters. This page lets you drag things around and see how geometry changes when Euclid's parallel postulate fails.

## How to run it

No installation is needed. It is a single HTML file with no dependencies.

1. Open the live link, **or**
2. Download `poincare-disk.html` and double-click it to open it in any modern browser.

(Fonts load from Google Fonts when online. The page falls back to system fonts offline, and everything else runs locally.)

## The six sections

| Tab | What to try |
|---|---|
| **Triangles** | Drag the corners. The angle sum is always under 180°, and the area equals π minus the angle sum. Try the target-angle challenge. |
| **Parallel lines** | Drag point P and the endpoints of line L. Infinitely many lines through P miss L (shown in gold). The two bold ones are the limiting parallels. |
| **Tilings** | Choose any regular tiling {p, q} with the sliders. Drag the disk to slide the plane around. Needs (p − 2)(q − 2) > 4. |
| **Flat vs. hyperbolic** | Compares your triangle with a flat triangle that has the same side lengths. |
| **Distance & circles** | Measure hyperbolic distance. Circumference is 2π sinh ρ and area is 2π(cosh ρ − 1). |
| **Proof walkthrough** | A five-step proof that the orthogonal circle through two points is the hyperbolic line through them. |

## The math behind it

- **Distance:** for points a and b, d(a, b) = 2 artanh |(b − a) / (1 − ā b)|.
- **Isometries** of the disk are Möbius transformations. Dragging the tiling applies one, and the code keeps it as a normalized 2×2 complex matrix.
- **Geodesics:** the circle through a and b orthogonal to the unit circle has center c satisfying 2 c·a = |a|² + 1 and 2 c·b = |b|² + 1.
- **Reflections** across a geodesic are inversions in that circle (or ordinary reflections across a diameter). The tilings are generated by repeatedly reflecting a central polygon across its edges.
- **Central polygon of {p, q}:** its vertices sit at Euclidean radius tanh(R/2), where cosh R = cot(π/p) · cot(π/q).
- **Area of a triangle:** π − (α + β + γ), for curvature −1.

## How it is built

Plain HTML, CSS, and JavaScript, with everything drawn on an HTML canvas. Complex numbers are stored as `[re, im]` pairs.
